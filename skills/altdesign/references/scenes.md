# The moving scene: how the hero is made

Studied from 17 short screen recordings Elaya (@elayadesigns) posted on X between Oct and Nov 2026, 7 to 15 s each, watched frame by frame (1.5 to 3 frames a second). Most say how they were made: an image model, an image to video model (Higgsfield, Kling 3, Julius), then Claude or Cursor with her landing page skill. The most liked one (11,207 likes) is a montage of 14 pages "designed in the last two months".

Her portfolio stills are in `portfolio.md`. The videos add what stills cannot show: **the picture moves, and it moves in one small way that belongs to the headline.** That, more than type or spacing, is what makes these pages look made by a designer.

## What every page has

1. **The first screen is one picture, full bleed.** It fills the viewport edge to edge (or a single framed card with 24 px margin). There is no second visual competing with it. Product UI, when it is there, is small: a code card, a prompt box, numbers printed on the sky, a dashboard that peeks 80 px up from the bottom edge.
2. **The picture is a pun on the headline.** Read the headline, then the picture, and the joke lands in one second.
3. **It has a named craft style.** Never a gradient, never a stock photo, never a screenshot collage.
4. **It moves.** A seamless loop of 6 to 10 s. The camera does not move. One thing in the scene does, and that thing is the pun.
5. **The UI is tiny and calm.** Heading 2 lines, 40 to 60 px. One line of subcopy at 14 px. One or two pill buttons. A quiet proof row at the bottom edge over the art (logos, stars, avatars, three stats with hairline dividers). The art takes 70% or more of the attention.
6. **Colour comes from the art.** The page ground, type tint and button are pulled from the picture. Most scenes are two tone (blue ink on cream, navy and orange, black and green) plus one pop.

## The rules this gives

1. **Write the pun before anything else.** Headline in 8 words or fewer, then: "the picture shows ___ doing ___". If the picture does not make the headline funnier or truer, change one of them. The headline can be bent to fit a good picture ("Let the claw catch the right fish" for an agent product).
2. **One subject, one action.** A lighthouse that sweeps, a crab that carries, a centurion who raises a sword. Not a crowd of ideas.
3. **Name the style in two words** (blue etching, anime sky, watercolour ink, stippled gouache, pixel painting, photoreal macro, halftone print) and keep it for every picture on the site: hero, footer, 404, section art.
4. **Character beats object.** A small character (block robot, frog, crab, astronaut) is remembered; give it the brand's colour and reuse it on the 404 and footer.
5. **Frame through an object of the domain** when the product is about seeing or going: cockpit window, container doors, an open window. The frame becomes the text container.
6. **Leave the copy a quiet zone.** Generate the still with empty sky, wall or floor where the heading sits (left third or top centre). Check contrast on the real frame, at the loop's brightest moment.
7. **Motion is small, slow and loops.** Static camera. One element moves; a second ambient one at most (clouds, rain, steam). 6 to 10 s, first frame equals last frame. Nothing in the UI layer loops.
8. **Layer the scene with the UI.** At least one of: art passes in front of UI (clouds over the numbers, the beam over the heading); UI peeks out of the art (dashboard rising from the bottom edge); the scene stays fixed while content scrolls over it. This is what makes it one page and not a picture with text on it.
9. **Load in after the art.** The scene is there first; the copy and the proof row blur in or fade in 300 to 600 ms later. A character can walk in on the first loop and then hold.
10. **The UI layer is small.** Heading 40 to 60 px, subcopy 14 to 16 px, pill buttons 32 to 40 px high, a proof row at the bottom. The scene is the scale.
11. **Below the fold the product shows itself**: bento cards where each card is a tiny working UI (cursor moves, line redraws, counter ticks, status lights), with believable specifics. This is where screenshots and real output belong.
12. **End on a scene too.** The footer and 404 reuse the style and the character.

## Making one

1. **Pun and style**: headline, the subject doing its action, the style, the two colours from the brand plus the pop, where the copy sits.
2. **Still**: an image model at 16:9, 2x the largest width shown (at least 2560 px wide, or upscale). Prompt shape: `[style], [subject] [doing its action], [setting], [two colours] with [pop] only on [one detail], [quiet zone: empty sky in the left third], no text, no logos`. Make 4 to 8 and pick by the pun, not by polish. Make a 9:16 crop or second still for phones.
3. **Animate**: an image to video model from the still. Prompt only the motion: `static camera, the [one element] [moves how], everything else still, seamless loop`. Use the still as the first and last frame when the model allows it, or cross fade the last 0.5 s into the first.
4. **Check the loop**: watch it 5 times. No morphing faces or hands, no new objects, no text appearing, no jump at the seam. If the subject changes shape, cut the clip shorter or lower the motion.
5. **Encode**: H.264 MP4 and WebM, muted, about 1 to 3 MB at 1920 wide, a 720 wide phone file under 1 MB, a WebP poster from the first frame. `autoplay muted loop playsinline`, poster set, loaded in view. Under reduced motion, show the poster only.
6. **Compose**: the video as a full bleed background (`object-fit: cover`, focal point set with `object-position`), a readability overlay only where the copy sits, then the copy, buttons and proof row. Then the layer move from rule 8.

## What our first demos got wrong (Nov 2026)

Our taktek.io and taktekbot.com demos passed every check and the owner rejected both. The hero was a pixel heading beside screenshots in windows: proof, but no scene, no pun, no motion, and the screenshots competed with each other. Proof belongs below the fold; the first screen belongs to one moving picture.
